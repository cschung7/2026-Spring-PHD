01 / Motivation
Why Networks?

Finance is a Network Problem

Traditional Finance

  • CAPM, APT — assets priced independently
  • Covariance matrix is "flat" — no structure
  • Diversification assumes pairwise correlations
  • Misses higher-order dependencies
"The financial system is a network of promises. When one node breaks a promise, the cascade is governed by network topology." — Haldane & May (2011), Nature

Network Finance

  • Nodes = banks, stocks, funds, countries
  • Edges = loans, correlations, exposures, ownership
  • Captures indirect linkages and contagion paths
  • Identifies systemic importance structurally

Key References

  • Mantegna (1999) — correlation-based stock networks
  • Allen & Gale (2000) — financial contagion
  • Billio et al. (2012) — Granger-causality networks
  • Diebold & Yılmaz (2014) — variance decomposition

04 / Centrality
Centrality

Which Institution is Most Important?

Degree

CD(i) = di / (n-1)
  • Counts: direct connections
  • Finance: diversification difficulty
  • High = hard to avoid in portfolio

Betweenness

CB(i) = Σ σst(i)/σst
  • Counts: shortest paths through i
  • Finance: contagion bottleneck
  • High = systemic risk intermediary

Closeness

CC(i) = (n-1) / Σ d(i,j)
  • Measures: avg distance to all
  • Finance: shock propagation speed
  • High = infects market fastest

Eigenvector

Ax = λ₁x
xi ∝ Σ Aijxj
  • Recursive: important if neighbors important
  • Finance: too-connected-to-fail
  • High = inner circle of the market
"Different centrality measures answer different policy questions: degree for exposure limits, betweenness for circuit breakers, eigenvector for bailout priority." — Battiston et al. (2012), Journal of Financial Economics

06 / Centrality
Eigenvector

Eigenvector Centrality & PageRank

Eigenvector Centrality:
A · x = λ₁ · x

xi = (1/λ₁) Σj Aij · xj

"My importance = sum of my neighbors'
importances, normalized by spectral radius"

Perron-Frobenius Theorem:
For connected, non-negative A:
• λ₁ is the largest eigenvalue
• x has all positive entries
• x is unique (up to scaling)

Financial Meaning

  • A stock is "important" if co-held with other important stocks
  • Captures the "inner circle" of institutional portfolios
  • High eigenvector = too-connected-to-fail
  • Its failure cascades through recursively important neighbors

PageRank — Damped Eigenvector

PageRank:
π = α · ATD-1 · π + (1-α) · 1/n

α = 0.85 (damping factor)
(1-α) = "random teleportation" probability

Finance interpretation:
α = investor follows correlations
1-α = investor randomly picks a stock

As α → 1: PageRank → Eigenvector centrality

Katz Centrality

x = (I - αA)-1 · 1
= Σk=0∞ αk Ak · 1

Counts ALL walks of ALL lengths
Decayed by αk (must be α < 1/λ₁)

Finance: indirect contagion channels
Every intermediary path counts, not just shortest
07 / Laplacian
Laplacian

The Graph Laplacian

Definition:
L = D − A

D = degree matrix (diagonal)
A = adjacency matrix

Properties:
• Symmetric (for undirected graphs)
• Positive semi-definite: all λi ≥ 0
• Row sums = 0: L · 1 = 0
• Smallest eigenvalue λ₀ = 0 always

Quadratic Form:
xTLx = Σ(i,j)∈E wij(xi − xj)²
= total "stress" across all edges
= smoothness penalty for signal x on graph

Financial Interpretation

  • L encodes how shocks diffuse through the network
  • xTLx = how much a portfolio allocation x is penalized by network structure
  • Smooth x (similar values on connected nodes) → low xTLx
  • The eigenvalues of L reveal network fragility

Normalized Laplacian

Symmetric normalized:
Lsym = D-½ L D-½ = I − D-½AD-½

Random walk:
Lrw = D-1L = I − D-1A

Normalization removes heterogeneity in node sizes
Critical when comparing US ($trillions) with KR ($billions)
08 / Laplacian
Algebraic Connectivity

λ₂ — The Fiedler Value

λ₂

Second smallest eigenvalue of L = algebraic connectivity

What λ₂ Measures

  • λ₂ > 0 ⟺ graph is connected
  • Larger λ₂ → more robust network (harder to split)
  • Smaller λ₂ → more fragile (a few cuts disconnect it)
  • λ₂ = 0 → network is already disconnected

Cheeger Inequality

λ₂ / 2 ≤ h(G) ≤ √(2·λ₂)

h(G) = Cheeger constant = min-cut / vol
Tight bound between spectral and combinatorial
Finance: h(G) = the narrowest bottleneck
in the financial system's contagion capacity
Sovereign debt network colored by Fiedler vector sign.
Positive (blue) vs Negative (red) = natural fault line.
09 / Laplacian
Spectral Partition

The Fiedler Vector

Fiedler Vector:
L · v₂ = λ₂ · v₂

v₂ = eigenvector for 2nd smallest eigenvalue

Spectral Bisection:
Group A = {i : v₂(i) ≥ 0}
Group B = {i : v₂(i) < 0}

This minimizes the normalized cut:
Ncut = cut(A,B)/vol(A) + cut(A,B)/vol(B)

Relaxation of the NP-hard min-cut problem
Solved exactly by eigendecomposition!

Finance Application

  • Partition sovereign debt network → find natural fault lines
  • Sign of v₂ reveals which countries form a bloc
  • Magnitude of v₂(i) → how strongly i belongs to its bloc
  • Near-zero v₂(i) → country is on the boundary (swing state)

Kirchhoff's Theorem

Number of spanning trees:
τ(G) = (1/n) · Πi=1n-1 λi

More spanning trees = more redundant paths

11 / Diffusion
Contagion

Heat Diffusion & Shock Propagation

Heat Equation on Graph:
∂u/∂t = −L · u

Solution:
u(t) = exp(−tL) · u(0)

u(0) = initial shock vector
u(t) = shock distribution at time t

Eigendecomposition:
u(t) = Σk exp(−λkt) · (vkTu₀) · vk

Each eigenmode decays at rate λk
λ₂ governs the slowest non-trivial decay

Lehman Brothers (2008)

  • Initial shock: u(0) = [0,...,1,...,0] at Lehman
  • t=0.001: shock reaches AIG, GS (direct CDS exposure)
  • t=0.01: shock spreads to all US banks
  • t=0.1: approaches stationary — entire system affected

12 / Random Walks
Random Walks

Random Walks on Financial Networks

Transition Matrix:
P = D−1A
Pij = Aij / di

"Probability of walking from i to j
proportional to edge weight"

Stationary Distribution:
πi = di / (2 · total_weight)

For undirected graphs: degree-proportional
π = where a random walker spends most time

Mean Return Time:
E[Ti→i] = 1/πi
High-degree nodes: short return → "attractors"

Financial Interpretation

  • Pij = probability a shock at i propagates to j next
  • πi = long-run fraction of risk absorbed by institution i
  • Institutions with high π are risk sinks
  • Mean return time 1/πi = how quickly risk cycles back

Communicability

Estrada Communicability:
Gij = (exp(A))ij = Σk=0∞ (Ak)ij / k!

Counts ALL walks, weighted by 1/k!
Captures TOTAL indirect exposure

Finance: two institutions may have
small direct exposure but massive
indirect exposure through intermediaries

Effective Resistance

  • Reff(i,j) = (ei−ej)T L+ (ei−ej)
  • Low Reff = many redundant paths = robust link
  • Kirchhoff index = Σ Reff = total system fragility
13 / Communicability
Deep Dive

Why exp(A) = Communicability

From Walk Counts to Matrix Exponential

  • We know Ak[i][j] = number of walks of length k from i to j
  • Sum them all at once, with a length penalty:
[eA]ij = Σk=0∞ (Ak)ij / k!

= Σ (length-k walk count) / k!

k! in the denominator is the key:
Longer walks are exponentially discounted
→ direct connections dominate the sum

Why k! and not something else?

  • Naive sum Σ Ak diverges when spectral radius λ₁ > 1
  • Katz uses αk — but requires tuning α < 1/λ₁
  • k! guarantees convergence for any matrix A
  • Information-theoretic meaning: "info transmitted through k intermediaries is diluted by k! possible orderings"

Spectral Decomposition — The Deeper View

Eigendecomposition:
A = Q Λ QT

Therefore:
exp(A) = Q · diag(eλ₁, eλ₂, ...) · QT

Element-wise:
[exp(A)]ij = Σk eλk · qki · qkj

Each eigenvector mode contributes
weighted by eλₖ

Why This Matters

  • Modes with large eigenvalues = core network structures
  • They get exponentially amplified by eλₖ
  • The dominant eigenvector mode controls communicability
  • λ₂ (algebraic connectivity) → larger λ₂ means the entire graph has higher communicability

Finance Implication

  • Two banks may have zero direct exposure
  • But massive indirect exposure through walk chains
  • exp(A) captures what the adjacency matrix misses
  • This is why regulators care about network topology, not just bilateral links

15 / Community
Modularity

Modularity as a Regime Indicator

Modularity:
Q = (1/2m) Σij [Aij − didj/(2m)] · δ(ci, cj)

Aij − didj/(2m) = deviation from random graph
δ(ci, cj) = 1 if same community

Interpretation:
Q ∈ [−0.5, 1]
Q > 0.3 → significant community structure
Q ≈ 0 → no structure (random graph)
Q < 0 → anti-community (bipartite-like)

Market Stress → Q Collapse

  • Normal times: Q ≈ 0.5 — clear sector clustering
  • Stress: cross-sector correlations rise
  • Crisis: Q → 0 — everything correlated
  • "Correlations go to 1 in a crash"
  • Monitoring Q over time → regime change detector
Normal market: clear sector clusters, Q ≈ 0.52

Louvain Algorithm

  • Phase 1: greedily assign nodes to communities that maximize ΔQ
  • Phase 2: collapse communities into super-nodes
  • Repeat until no improvement → O(n log n)
  • The most widely used community detection method
16 / Synthesis — Adjacency
Matrix A

Adjacency Matrix: Who is Connected

Think of A as the phone book of the financial system. Aij ≠ 0 means "bank i and bank j have a direct relationship." Everything below is derived from this one table.

Click the buttons to explore each concept visually.

① Ak → Walk Counts

(A²)ij = Σm Aim · Amj
= number of 2-step paths from i to j
  • Analogy: "How many ways can a rumor travel from JPM to Citi through exactly one intermediary?"
  • In finance: A² counts all indirect bilateral exposures through one intermediary bank
  • A³ = exposures through two intermediaries — these are the "hidden" contagion channels regulators miss
  • Why it matters: Lehman had small direct exposure to many banks, but massive A² and A³ indirect paths through AIG

② Eigenvectors → Centrality

A · x = λ₁ · x
xi = importance of node i
  • Analogy: "Who is popular among the popular?" — like asking which CEO is most respected by other respected CEOs
  • Unlike simple degree (= count of friends), eigenvector centrality says: it matters WHO your friends are
  • A bank connected to 3 systemically important banks is more dangerous than one connected to 10 small ones
  • Why eigenvectors? Because the equation Ax = λx is self-consistent — the only vector where "sum of neighbors' importance" equals "scaled self-importance" is the dominant eigenvector

③ exp(A) → Communicability

[eA]ij = Σk=0∞ (Ak)ij / k!
= all walk counts, discounted by length
  • Analogy: "Total ability for information to flow from i to j through every possible chain, with longer chains heavily discounted"
  • Walk counts (Ak) tell you paths exist. Communicability tells you how much actually gets through
  • Like the telephone game — the message degrades with each relay. k! discount = information loss through k intermediaries
  • Why it matters: Two banks with zero direct exposure can have huge communicability through shared intermediaries — this is the hidden systemic risk A alone doesn't show

④ Encodes Direct Structure

Aij ≠ 0 ⟺ direct relationship
The "raw data" of the entire network
  • Analogy: A is like the balance sheet of the financial system — every bilateral relationship in one table
  • From A alone, you can derive: degree (how connected?), density (how interconnected is the whole system?), clustering (do my neighbors know each other?)
  • A is the foundation — L and P are both derived from A. If you understand A, you understand the raw wiring of the system
  • Key limitation: A only shows who is directly connected. The real danger is in indirect paths — that's why we need Ak, exp(A), and the other matrices
17 / Synthesis — Laplacian
Matrix L

Laplacian: How Things Flow

If A is the road map, L = D − A is the traffic flow model. It encodes not just connections, but how shocks, money, and information propagate through those connections.

Click the buttons to explore each concept visually.

① λ₂ → Algebraic Connectivity

L has eigenvalues 0 = λ₀ ≤ λ₁ ≤ ... ≤ λn
λ₂ = how hard it is to split the network
  • Analogy: Imagine the network is a bridge. λ₂ measures how many cables you'd have to cut before it collapses into two pieces
  • λ₂ = 0 means the network is already split — the financial system has fragmented into isolated blocs
  • Large λ₂ = resilient. Small λ₂ = one failure away from fragmentation
  • Why economists care: λ₂ dropping over time → early warning signal. Like watching credit spreads widen — the network is under structural stress

② v₂ → Network Partition

L · v₂ = λ₂ · v₂
Sign of v₂(i) assigns node i to a group
  • Analogy: Imagine stretching a rubber network and seeing where it naturally wants to tear. The Fiedler vector v₂ reveals that tear line
  • v₂(i) > 0 → node i is in Group A. v₂(i) < 0 → Group B. The network itself tells you the natural partition
  • In sovereign debt: v₂ might split "Asia+Emerging" from "Europe+US" — revealing the fault line that would crack during a crisis
  • Why it's remarkable: This is the NP-hard "min-cut" problem solved exactly by computing a single eigenvector. Linear algebra gives you what combinatorics can't

③ exp(−tL) → Diffusion

u(t) = exp(−tL) · u(0)
Shock at t=0 spreads according to L
  • Analogy: Drop a drop of ink into water. It spreads outward — fast at first, then slows. The Laplacian governs exactly this spread on a network
  • u(0) = "Lehman defaults" (shock vector). u(t) = how that shock has spread to every bank at time t
  • The spread rate is governed by λ₂: large λ₂ → ink spreads quickly (fast contagion). Small λ₂ → ink stays localized (isolated clusters)
  • The paradox: a robust network (high λ₂) means shocks spread faster. Robustness against fragmentation = vulnerability to contagion

④ Encodes Flow Structure

xTLx = Σ(i,j)∈E wij(xi − xj)²
= total "disagreement" across edges
  • Analogy: Assign each bank a "temperature" (its stress level). xTLx measures the total heat difference across all connections — how "uneven" is stress in the system?
  • If connected banks have similar stress → xTLx is small (stress is "smooth"). If adjacent banks have wildly different stress → xTLx is large (stress is "rough")
  • This is the smoothness penalty: L tells you which signals are "natural" on this network (smooth) vs "forced" (rough)
  • Portfolio insight: a portfolio allocation x has low xTLx when correlated assets get similar weights — the Laplacian encodes which allocations "respect" the correlation structure
18 / Synthesis — Transition Matrix
Definition

What is the Transition Matrix?

The transition matrix converts the adjacency matrix (raw connections) into a probability machine. It answers: "If a shock is at bank i right now, where does it go next?"

Click the buttons to see each aspect of the transition matrix.

From Adjacency to Probability

Definition:
P = D−1A

Pij = Aij / deg(i)

D = diagonal matrix of node degrees
Each row of P sums to 1 (probability)
  • A counts walks: "How many length-k paths exist from i to j?"
  • P gives probabilities: "If a random walker is at i, what's the chance it steps to j?"
  • The difference is normalization — a bank with 10 connections sends 1/10 of its shock to each neighbor; a bank with 2 connections sends 1/2
  • This is a Markov chain: the next state depends only on the current state, not the history

Why Divide by Degree?

Without normalization (A):
JPM row: [0, 12.5, 8.3, 0, 0, 0]
→ total = 20.8 (not a probability!)

With normalization (P = D⁻¹A):
JPM row: [0, 0.60, 0.40, 0, 0, 0]
→ total = 1.0 ✓ (valid probability)
  • Analogy: JPM has $20.8B in loans to two banks. If JPM gets a shock, it distributes proportionally: 60% to GS ($12.5B link), 40% to Citi ($8.3B link)
  • A high-degree node (many connections) dilutes its shock across many neighbors — each neighbor gets a small fraction
  • A low-degree node (few connections) concentrates its shock — each neighbor gets a large fraction
  • This is why degree matters: not all connections are equally dangerous

Stationary Distribution: πP = π

Definition:
π · P = π (left eigenvector, eigenvalue = 1)

Solution (undirected graphs):
π(i) = deg(i) / Σj deg(j)

π is proportional to degree!
  • Meaning: after infinitely many random walk steps, the fraction of time spent at node i converges to π(i) — regardless of where you started
  • Why degree? High-degree nodes have more "incoming roads." A random walker entering any intersection is more likely to stumble into a node with many connections
  • Finance: in the interbank network, risk naturally accumulates at the most connected institutions. π tells regulators where to put the biggest capital buffers
  • Return time: E[Ti→i] = 1/π(i) — highly connected banks see risk cycle back to them faster

Two Normalizations = Two Philosophies

The difference is not just formulas — it's how you aggregate neighbor information.

Example: A(deg=3) connected to B,C,D (each deg=1)

D⁻¹A — "내가 보내는 쪽만 정규화"
hA = (1/3)(hB + hC + hD) ← A의 degree로만 나눔
hB = (1/1)(hA) ← B는 A 전체를 그대로 받음!

D⁻½AD⁻½ — "보내는 쪽과 받는 쪽 양쪽 모두 정규화"
hA = (1/√3)(hB/√1 + hC/√1 + hD/√1) ← 양쪽 degree 고려
hB = (1/√1)(hA/√3) ← A의 degree도 분모에 반영
Edge (i,j) weightMeaning
D⁻¹A1 / deg(i)i의 선택지 수만큼 희석
D⁻½AD⁻½1 / √(deg(i)·deg(j))양쪽 degree의 기하평균으로 희석
  • D⁻¹A 문제: 스케일 불균형 — leaf 노드 B는 허브 A의 정보를 100% 그대로 받지만, A는 이웃을 희석해서 받음
  • D⁻½AD⁻½ 장점: 균형 + 허브 패널티 — B도 A를 1/√3 비율로 받아 허브의 과도한 영향력을 구조적으로 억제
  • Properties: D⁻¹A는 row-stochastic (각 행의 합 = 1). D⁻½AD⁻½는 symmetric → 고유값이 항상 실수, spectral 분석 가능
  • Finance: 허브(degree 높은 종목)가 많은 그래프에서 D⁻¹A를 쓰면 leaf 노드에 거의 희석 없이 전달됨. D⁻½AD⁻½는 허브의 영향력을 자동 감쇠시켜 spillover 분석 결과에 상당한 차이를 만듦
19 / P — Stationary Distribution
Matrix P ①

Stationary π → Risk Sinks

Circle size ∝ π. Yellow dot = random walker. JPM & AIG have highest π — system's risk sinks.

Stationary Distribution π

Definition: π · P = π
πi = di / 2m
Long-run fraction of time spent at node i
  • Analogy: Imagine a drunk walker stumbling randomly through the banking network, choosing the next bank proportional to exposure size. After millions of steps, πi = fraction of time spent at bank i
  • High πi means bank i is a "gravity well" — risk keeps getting pulled back there regardless of where it starts
  • In financial networks: π is proportional to total exposure. The biggest, most connected banks absorb the most long-run risk
  • Policy insight: π identifies which banks need the largest capital buffers — not because they caused the crisis, but because risk always flows toward them
  • Return time: E[Ti→i] = 1/π(i) — highly connected banks see risk cycle back to them faster
20 / P — Mean First Passage Time
Matrix P ②

MFPT → Contagion Speed

노드 i에서 출발한 random walker가 처음으로 노드 j에 도달할 때까지 걸리는 평균 걸음 수

1D chain: H(1→4)=9 걸음. 재귀식 + Lehman ripple이 6초마다 교차.

MFPT = Mean First Passage Time

공식 정의:
H(i→j) = E[min{t ≥ 1 : Xt=j} | X0=i]

재귀 방정식 (핵심):
H(i,j) = 1 + Σk≠j P[i][k] · H(k,j)
한 걸음(+1) 후, 이웃 k→j의 MFPT를 가중합산

행렬 해법:
Z = (I − P + W)−1 (fundamental matrix)
H(i,j) = (Z[j][j] − Z[i][j]) / π(j)
H(i,i) = 1/π(i) (귀환 시간 = 역수)

Key Properties

  • 비대칭: H(i→j) ≠ H(j→i) — 허브에게 "가는 시간"은 짧지만, 허브가 "나를 찾아오는 시간"은 훨씬 김
  • Commute time: C(i,j) = H(i→j) + H(j→i). Spectral: C = V·Σ (uk(i)−uk(j))²/λk
  • vs Hop count: MFPT는 구조 전체 반영 — 병목(bottleneck) 있으면 MFPT 폭발
  • Finance: λ₂ 작을수록 commute time↑ → 그래프 취약
21 / P — Spectral Gap
Matrix P ③

Spectral Gap → Mixing Time

Two regimes alternating: small gap (bottleneck) vs large gap (well-mixed).

Spectral Gap & Mixing

Definition:
gap = 1 − |λ₂(P)|
mixing time ∝ 1/gap
  • Analogy: Stir cream into coffee. Few channels → slow mixing (cream stays separate). Well-connected → fast mixing (cream blends instantly). The spectral gap measures this speed
  • Large gap → the network "forgets" the initial shock quickly — contagion reaches everywhere fast, then equalizes
  • Small gap → bottlenecks. Shocks stay trapped in one part, slowly leaking. Two "worlds" that barely communicate
  • Market regime: Normal times → small gap (distinct sectors). Crisis → gap widens (everything correlates → one big pot of risk)

Connection to Laplacian

  • Gap of P = λ₂ of normalized Laplacian Lrw
  • Same number, different matrix — P and L are two sides of the same coin
22 / P — Dynamics
Matrix P ④

P Encodes Dynamics

Step-by-step: u(t) = Pt·u(0). Watch risk redistribute. Eventually → stationary π.

Markov Chain Dynamics

u(t+1) = P · u(t)
Risk distribution evolves step by step
  • Analogy: A and L are photographs — they show the network at one instant. P is a movie — it shows how things evolve over time
  • Given any initial shock u(0), multiply by P repeatedly: u(1) = Pu(0), u(2) = P²u(0), ... This traces the exact path of contagion
  • P converges to π regardless of starting point — all roads lead to the same long-run equilibrium

The Complete Picture

  • A tells you the wiring — who is connected
  • L tells you the flow capacity — where it cracks
  • P tells you the actual dynamics — where risk is right now, and where it will be tomorrow
  • All three from one network. Three questions, three matrices, one reality

All derived from A:
